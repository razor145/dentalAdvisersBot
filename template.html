The 3 Most Common Efficiency Killers

 

 

Based on your answers, here is how your system performs against the three issues we see most often:

 

⚡  Efficiency Killer #1 — The On/Off Mistake

The biggest mistake hardworking families make is switching the heating off at night. Don't do it. A heat pump isn't a sprinter; it’s a marathon runner. It’s designed to hum along at a steady 18°C–21°C all day and night. Keep it on, let it run low and slow.

 

⚡  Efficiency Killer #2 — Don’t "Choke" Your Radiators

You might think you’re saving electricity by turning off radiators in the spare room, but you’re actually choking the system. Your heat pump needs the heating system water to flow freely, to work properly. Shutting radiator valves (TRVs), forces the pump to "short-cycle"—turning on and off every few minutes like a car stuck in traffic. This wears out the heat pump and sends your electric meter spinning.
Keep those radiator valves wide open for a warm house and fair bills.

 

⚡  Efficiency Killer #3 — Hot Water Tank Immersion Overuse

Hidden inside your water tank is a backup immersion heater. It’s just like a power- hungry electric kettle. Your heat pump is perfectly capable of heating your bath water. The only time the immersion should kick in is once a week to kill off any legionella bugs. If yours is running every day, your hot water is more expensive than it needs to be. Check your settings and make sure the pump is in charge.

 

Your Simple Action List

 

 

These are the quick wins we recommend you try first. They cost nothing and can make a noticeable difference to your comfort and bills:

 

•          Increase clearance around the outdoor unit to at least 300mm on all sides, especially the fan outlet, by modifying or removing the surrounding structure.

•          Verify hot water cylinder temperature settings to ensure it's not set excessively high and that an immersion heater (if present) is not activating unnecessarily.

•          Check the outdoor unit — inspect the rear of the unit for any ice build-up. Some frosting is normal in cold weather, but a thick layer of ice that doesn't clear within an hour or two could indicate a defrost fault.

•          Schedule a professional system health check and commissioning review by an MCS-certified engineer to ensure optimal performance and identify any minor inefficiencies.

•          Check your hot water cylinder settings — if your 'Boost' or immersion heater is running daily, consider reducing the frequency or temperature to the minimum safe level (60°C).
